## Legacy (Centralized) Finance vs Decentralized Finance

**Centralized finance (CeFi)** relies on intermediaries: banks, brokers, and exchanges hold your funds and execute transactions on your behalf. You depend on their systems, their rules, and their solvency. That can mean:

- Higher fees and hidden costs  
- Limited access depending on geography or status  
- Opaque processes and delayed settlements  
- Counterparty risk (if the intermediary fails, your access can too)

**Decentralized finance (DeFi)** is built on **transparency, accessibility, and user control**. With DeFi:

- You connect with **your own wallet**; no single company holds your keys.  
- Transactions are recorded on public blockchains and can be verified by anyone.  
- Protocols are often open source and composable, so developers can build on top of them.  
- Access is permissionless: if you have a wallet and internet, you can use DeFi apps (subject to local laws).

DexKit’s DeFi products are designed to work with **self-custody wallets**. Users keep full control of their assets while still enjoying swap tools, NFT features, and custom DApp experiences. Solutions like [Magic (ERC-4337 / wallet abstraction)](https://magic.link/) can simplify onboarding while still allowing users to export their keys and move to a wallet they fully control—aligning with DexKit’s focus on user sovereignty.

## CEX vs DEX: Why Both Matter

**Centralized exchanges (CEXs)** have dominated crypto trading for years. They offer familiar interfaces, fiat on-ramps, and customer support. But they also concentrate risk: hacks, insider issues, and regulatory crackdowns can lock or wipe user funds. History is full of examples—from Mt. Gox to more recent exchange collapses.

**Decentralized exchanges (DEXs)** let users trade **directly from their wallets** via smart contracts. No single entity holds your crypto; you sign transactions and the protocol executes the swap. That reduces counterparty risk and supports true self-custody. The trade-off is that you are responsible for your keys, transaction settings, and security—so education and good habits matter. Our guide on [Simple Steps to Keep Your Crypto Safe](https://dexkit.com/blog/simple-steps-to-keep-your-crypto-safe/) and [Crypto Mastery: Navigating Risks and Riding High in the Investment Game](https://dexkit.com/blog/crypto-mastery-navigating-risks-and-riding-high-in-the-investment-game/) can help you stay protected.

In practice, many users combine both: CEXs for onboarding and some trading, DEXs for self-custody and DeFi. For a deeper comparison, see [CEX vs DEX: Learn Why You Need Both](https://dexkit.com/blog/cex-vs-dex-learn-why-you-need-both/).

## DeFi’s Current Challenges

DeFi is powerful but not risk-free. Important challenges include:

- **Complexity:** Managing keys, gas fees, and multiple chains can be overwhelming for newcomers.  
- **Smart contract risk:** Bugs or exploits can lead to loss of funds; audits and caution are essential.  
- **Lack of traditional support:** There is no central “customer service” to reverse a mistaken transaction.  
- **Regulation:** Rules are still evolving and may affect which products are available in your jurisdiction.

Despite this, the ecosystem keeps improving: better UX, Layer 2 scaling for lower fees, and more robust protocols. For a sobering look at what can go wrong, you can review external roundups such as [major DeFi hacks and exploits](https://www.hedgewithcrypto.com/defi-hacks/) to learn from past incidents and prioritize security.

## DeFi Breaking New Ground: Layer 2 and DexKit’s Role

DeFi is no longer limited to Ethereum mainnet during peak congestion. **Layer 2 (L2) networks**—such as Polygon, Arbitrum, and Optimism—deliver faster, cheaper transactions while staying secured by or compatible with Ethereum. That unlocks:

- Affordable swaps and transfers for everyday users  
- Viable microtransactions and gaming economies  
- More experimentation by builders and digital entrepreneurs
